01 — Strategy

What Aurvium is, precisely.

Everything downstream — the mark, the palette, the way the invoice is worded — is a consequence of the decisions on this page. If a future piece of work contradicts something here, the work is wrong, not this page.

Category
Finance Architecture. Not a dashboard, not a bookkeeping service, not RevRec software. Aurvium is a studio that designs and builds the systems — revenue recognition, billing pipelines, ERP integration, MRR/ARR reporting — that a scaling company's finance function runs on permanently.
Who it's for
VC-backed SaaS companies roughly $5M–$30M ARR — past the point where spreadsheets and manual Stripe reconciliation hold up, before the company can justify hiring a full systems team in-house. The buyer is the first real finance hire: a Head of Finance or newly-appointed CFO who has to make the finance function board-ready and audit-ready, fast, without engineers to lean on.
The wedge
AlternativeWhy it falls short
Hiring in-houseSlow to recruit for, expensive to carry at this stage, and the first hire is usually generalist — not someone who has built ERP and revenue-recognition architecture before.
Outsourced bookkeeping / fractional CFO firmsMaintain the books that already exist. They don't design the systems those books depend on — so the underlying fragility never gets fixed, just monitored.
AurviumCloser to an architecture firm than an accounting firm. Engaged for a defined build — discovery, design, implementation — and leaves behind a permanent system, not a recurring service line.
Business model
Project-based engagements with a fixed scope and a defined deliverable — not a subscription. This is why every piece of collateral in this system is shaped like a proposal or an invoice, never a product dashboard.

02 — Identity / The Mark

Two halves, one channel.

The mark is a diamond split at its widest point — two solid forms held apart by a precise gold channel. It's the one piece of prior work strong enough to keep outright: the channel is the -vium in Aurvium, made visible rather than illustrated. What changed here is proportion, not concept — the gap was too tight in earlier drafts and has been opened, faded at both ends, and shifted slightly above true centre so the two halves read as equal weight to the eye.

02 — Identity / Typography

Gravitas, then precision.

Three roles, deliberately not four. The earlier direction paired the mark with Cormorant Garamond — a fine typeface, but one that reads as private-bank stationery, not a systems studio. DM Serif Display keeps the same warmth and weight without the antique, calligraphic edge. IBM Plex was built by IBM for enterprise engineering documentation — it is quite literally the correct register for numbers, hex values, and labels in a brand about architecture.

03 — Voice & Tone

Diagnose before you prescribe.

Aurvium speaks the language of CFOs but thinks like an engineer. It never hedges with "not a vendor, not a consultant" — that's a definition by negation, and it tells a buyer nothing. It says exactly what it is and what it costs to get wrong.

We say this
"We build the revenue recognition layer your auditors will actually trust."
Specific, names the audience that matters (the auditor, not the CFO's inbox), implies the outcome without promising magic.
Not this
"We transform your finance function with best-in-class solutions."
Vendor-speak. Says nothing a competitor couldn't also claim.
We say this
"Your MRR pipeline is breaking because the data layer was never built for scale. We fix that permanently."
Diagnoses before prescribing — shows understanding of the problem before offering anything.
Not this
"We'd love to help streamline your financial workflows!"
Eager to please. The exclamation mark alone disqualifies it.
04 — Collateral

05 — Appendix

What changed, and why.

For continuity with everything built before this document — three separate design sessions produced three incompatible "final" systems. Here is exactly what this version kept, cut, and fixed, and the reasoning for each call.

DecisionReasoning
Kept the split-diamond / channel markMost distinctive asset built across all three sessions, and the only one where the visual concept directly embodies the name's etymology (-vium = channel).
Cut Cormorant Garamond, adopted DM Serif Display + IBM PlexCormorant reads as private-bank / editorial luxury — the exact tonal risk flagged and rejected earlier in the same project when comparing logo directions. Never actually resolved before now.
Standardised on one gold, one full paletteEarlier files used four different gold hex values under the same swatch names across sessions.
Cut "not a vendor, not a consultant, not a software tool"Definition by negation. Replaced with a positive definition and a named competitive wedge.
Declared project-based engagements, not subscription SaaSResolves the contradiction between services-shaped collateral (invoices, proposals) built in one session and a self-referential SaaS dashboard mockup built in another. Dashboard dropped entirely.
Fixed invoice tableOld draft priced flat project fees through a Qty × Rate column, which only makes sense for hourly billing.
Standardised domain & contact formataurvium.com and n.alrashidi@aurvium.com had drifted to aurvium.io and inconsistent name spellings in parallel sessions.
